Operating Result Signal
The −$7.7M FY25 deficit followed a +$2.9M FY24 surplus — a $10.6M swing driven by personnel cost growth, reduced endowment distribution, and emergency capital expense. The structural trend points to a recurring gap, not a one-time miss.
Reserve Position
At 43 days cash, FAS unrestricted reserves are below the NACUBO 60-day best practice. Peer institutions (Yale: 67 days, Princeton: 80 days) maintain larger buffers. FAS Moody's Aaa is sustained by University-level backing, but FAS-level liquidity is thin.
Near-Term Opportunity
A $48.2M donor-intent unlock sprint could release dormant restricted funds into lawful mission-aligned spending. Combined with the $26M above-plan Continuing Education revenue and careful June close management, FY26 can close materially better than the YTD baseline.
📊 CFO Analyst Read — FY26 Close Posture
Hold the variance: The $20.4M favorable YTD position is predominantly timing-driven (vacancies, grant ramp delays). Bank it as contingency until June close accruals are complete — do not release for discretionary use.
Physics is the most acute risk: Third consecutive year of overrun; requires chair-level intervention, capital exception, and immediate postdoc-start freeze. Without action, this becomes a structural item in FY27.
Financial aid is the largest single exposure: The $12.2M above-plan demand is partly structural (affordability commitments) and partly enrollment-mix driven. Reforecast must be completed before FY27 budget is locked.
ICR recovery desk is the fastest revenue lever: Federal award timing creates a recoverable gap — but only with weekly billing, bridge-funding protocols, and active award reactivation coordination.

Net Tuition Compression — CFO Priority

Harvard College's financial aid commitment (no-loan policy, family contribution caps) means aid demand structurally tracks above gross tuition growth. Without the HES enrollment surge ($26M above plan), FY26 net tuition would be negative vs FY25. The CFO should model HES enrollment growth as a primary revenue lever and ensure all growth scenarios are tested before FY27 tuition rate decisions.
Aid Compression Risk
At 58.3% discount rate, FAS is below the NACUBO peer median of 61.4%, but the trend direction matters more than the current level. The discount rate has risen from 54.1% in FY22 to 58.3% in FY26 — a 4.2pp structural shift that erodes net yield each year.
HES as Strategic Lever
Harvard Extension School's 14,812 enrolled students (vs 14,200 plan) generated $26M above plan — the single largest favorable revenue offset in FY26. Every additional 1,000 HES enrollments adds ~$3.5M in net revenue. Mission-fit guardrails are needed.
Restricted Aid Offset
Identifying and deploying restricted endowment funds earmarked for financial aid can offset budget pressure without requiring gross tuition increases. The restricted-fund unlock sprint should prioritize donor-purpose analysis for aid-eligible endowments first.

Capital Triage Requirement — Board Cycle Preparation

The $842M deferred maintenance backlog must be triaged before the next Board cycle using four criteria: (1) life safety and code compliance, (2) research and teaching mission continuity, (3) escalation risk (problems that grow faster the longer they wait), and (4) reserve impact. Emergency repairs that escape capital governance and hit the operating budget — like the $12M FY25 emergency HVAC spend — are the primary risk to avoid.
Emergency Repair Risk
The $12M in emergency operating expenses in FY25 came directly from the deferred maintenance backlog — buildings that should have had planned capital work but received emergency repairs instead. This will recur unless a capital reserve policy and triage framework are established.
Reserve Draw Threshold
The capital reserve balance at $124M is below the $150M CFO threshold. Two simultaneous emergency repairs (like a HVAC failure and a structural failure) could deplete the reserve to a level that impairs routine capital planning.
Space Optimization Opportunity
Research labs cost $184/sqft/year — more than 4× the cost of classroom space. A space utilization audit across 40+ departments could identify labs operating below 60% utilization, creating consolidation options that reduce both maintenance and energy cost.
