Executive strategy & operational framework

Source control protects treatment, waterways and resource recovery

A board-level blueprint connecting Clean Water Act pretreatment obligations to operating capacity, cost recovery and PIMS-enabled accountability.

Strategic imperative

Stop toxic loading before it reaches the treatment plants.

Deer Island and Clinton are advanced wastewater treatment facilities, but they are not designed to remove every metal, petroleum product, industrial chemical or persistent organic compound. TRAC’s source-control mission protects biological treatment, collection infrastructure and workers; reduces pass-through risk to receiving waters; and helps preserve biosolids reuse through MWRA’s Bay State Fertilizer program.

Model method, AI governance and verified additions from the attached prototype

Forecast method. A transparent ensemble blends a five-year least-squares trend, a recent three-year trend, the median annual change and a no-change anchor. Trend effects are damped across FY26–FY30. “Control gain” and “Operational stress” are explicit planning assumptions, not learned outcomes.

Governance. Use NIST AI RMF’s Govern, Map, Measure and Manage functions: approve intended use and owners; document features and affected decisions; back-test and monitor error/drift; require human approval and a rollback path. Do not use this prototype to automate permits, SNC, enforcement or public claims.

Methods & sources

Transparent by design

Long-form documentation is folded to keep the main dashboard decision-focused.

Data definitions and transformation notes
  • The five-year trend uses MWRA Industrial Waste Reports for FY2021–FY2025, each covering July 1–June 30.
  • “SIUs” in executive KPIs is the greatest number designated during the fiscal year. “City SIUs” counts unique Facility IDs appearing in public Appendix A; Boston neighborhood address labels are normalized to Boston.
  • FY21–FY22 city totals are one below the executive maximum because the heat map uses unique facility IDs rather than maximum in-year status. FY23–FY25 reconcile exactly.
  • Industry-field counts are text matches to National Pretreatment Categorical Standards in Appendix A. One facility can match multiple fields. They are descriptive, not a complete industry universe.
  • The candidate forecast uses five annual observations and an interpretable damped ensemble. FY26 is a nowcast because it is complete but not represented in the public annual-report series; FY27 is the current operating-year forecast; FY30 is the fifth forecast endpoint.
  • The FY25 holdout check trains the same simplified ensemble on FY21–FY24 and reports the absolute error against FY25. It is a diagnostic, not a substitute for rolling validation on monthly data.
  • Scenario adjustments and uncertainty bands are documented planning assumptions. They are not probability statements, official MWRA targets or regulatory confidence limits. The CSV labels every forecast row as candidate-generated.
  • KPI target text distinguishes regulatory operating floors, directional planning lenses and context measures; the dashboard does not invent official MWRA numeric targets.
  • The peer matrix reviews affirmative evidence on official public pages for MWRA, King County, Portland and HRSD. “Not found” is not evidence of absence. Capability-prevalence counts exclude in-progress items and are not performance scores.
  • The SOPs, unified action register and tracker are candidate-proposed governance tools informed by EPA quality-system SOP guidance and control-authority responsibilities. Initial stages are planning baselines inferred from public evidence, not certified MWRA completion percentages.
  • All proposed SLAs, controls and action briefs are recommendations. No synthetic performance scores are mixed with reported program data.
